<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Schema.org 3.3 has been&nbsp;<a href="http://schema.org/docs/releases.html#v3.3">released</a>. As always, the release was prepared, debated and finalized by the&nbsp;<a href="https://www.w3.org/community/schemaorg/">schema.org community group</a>, and features a range of additions, adjustments, bugfixes and clarifications to improve the expressiveness and usability of our schemas.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">See the&nbsp;<a href="http://schema.org/docs/releases.html#v3.3">release notes</a>&nbsp;for full details, but of particular note are some changes made around the NewsArticle type (in collaboration with the&nbsp;<a href="http://www.thetrustproject.org/">Trust Project</a>&nbsp;on whose work this is largely based). For many years, our definition of NewsArticle was simply &#8220;a news article&#8221;. With this release we add (via our &#8220;pending&#8221; mechanism) some more subtlety around&nbsp;<a href="http://schema.org/docs/news.html">News</a>, making it possible to mark-up categories of news including opinion pieces, background articles, reportage, as well as as also introducing types for satirical and advertiser content. We also&nbsp;<a href="http://schema.org/docs/releases.html#g1525">add</a>&nbsp;properties that encourage greater transparency around News creation and publication. These are flagged as &#8220;pending&#8221; to emphasize that early adopter feedback on the new vocabulary is particularly welcomed, via&nbsp;<a href="http://github.com/schemaorg/schemaorg/issues">Github</a>, the W3C&nbsp;<a href="https://www.w3.org/community/schemaorg/">group</a>, or the site&#8217;s&nbsp;<a href="https://schema.org/docs/feedback.html">feedback form</a>. These developments complement our earlier work to support interoperability amongst fact-checking sites via the&nbsp;<a href="http://schema.org/ClaimReview">ClaimReview</a>&nbsp;type. Following discussion at&nbsp;<a href="http://about.poynter.org/node/102080">GlobalFact4 conference</a>, we have also&nbsp;<a href="https://github.com/schemaorg/schemaorg/issues/1687">amended</a>&nbsp;the definition of the &#8220;<a href="http://schema.org/expires">expires</a>&#8221; to highlight its applicability to fact checking content.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Other highlights of 3.3 include new terminology (also pending implementor feedback) for describing&nbsp;<a href="http://schema.org/docs/releases.html#g1156">legislation</a>,&nbsp;based on the&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/mdr/eli/">European Legislation Identifier (ELI) ontology</a>&nbsp;and the work of the&nbsp;<a href="http://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli-register/about.html">ELI taskforce</a>. We have also added an overview page giving more details on our&nbsp;<a href="http://schema.org/docs/financial.html">finance</a>-related terminology, contributed by the FIBO community, alongside a proposed design for describing&nbsp;<a href="http://schema.org/docs/releases.html#g1457">schedules</a>, new subtypes distinguishing user from critic reviews, and a generalization of our recipes schema called &#8220;<a href="http://schema.org/docs/releases.html#g647">HowTo</a>&#8221; for recipe-like tasks that don&#8217;t result in food. We&#8217;ve also added types for&nbsp;<a href="http://schema.org/docs/releases.html#g1459">TouristAttraction</a>&nbsp;and for&nbsp;<a href="http://schema.org/docs/releases.html#g1624">PublicToilet</a>&#8230;</p>
